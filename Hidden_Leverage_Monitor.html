After 2008 the risky lending left the banks. The official data lost sight of it.

Rules written after the crisis made risky loans expensive for banks. The lending moved to private credit funds, which are lightly supervised and report quarterly. The credit series our model uses shows lending outside banks shrinking. Bank loans to those funds show the opposite.

How it is packaged: 2006 next to 2026

Step2006: subprime mortgages2026: private credit and AI debt
Why it movedBanks parked loans in off-balance-sheet vehicles (SIVs, conduits) to hold less capital.Post-2008 capital rules and the 2013 leveraged-lending guidance pushed risky loans out of banks. Regulators said in Dec 2025 that this moved lending "outside of the regulatory perimeter".
BorrowersSubprime homeowners, 20% of new mortgages.Mid-sized buyout companies (75% earn under $100m a year; debt 5–7× earnings) and AI data centres (about $800bn of 2025–28 AI capex expected from private credit).
LenderMortgage originators such as New Century.Private credit funds and business development companies (BDCs): $2.6tn.
PackagingMortgage bonds re-packaged into CDOs with AAA ratings.Private-credit CLOs ($155bn, 16% of the US CLO market), rated notes sold to insurers, loans against fund portfolios (NAV loans), data-centre SPVs. Some ratings come from small agencies; the FSB reports ratings inflation.
Bank linkWarehouse lines and backstops to SIVs.Credit lines to funds: $1.7tn drawn. The top 5 banks hold 63% of lending commitments to BDCs.
Who carries the lossBanks, money market funds, AIG.Pension funds (42% of private debt fund shares), households (23%), life insurers (14%), annuity buyers at PE-owned insurers, retail buyers of semi-liquid funds (13% of BDC and registered fund assets).
How stress is hiddenRatings slow to move.Loans are valued quarterly by the manager; interest paid in more debt (PIK) on about 12% of loans; maturities extended instead of defaults.
First cracksNew Century fails (Apr 2007); Bear Stearns funds (Jul 2007).Tricolor and First Brands fail (Sep 2025); Blue Owl and Blackstone limit withdrawals (Feb and Jun 2026); BDC shares 15–33% below loan value.

Why the election matters here

Signal

Wages +3.2% y/y vs prices +3.7%, electricity +4.4%. Growth this narrow is what voters punished, in 2006 and now.

Policy shock

A Democratic Senate Banking Committee is expected to push stress tests of bank lending to private credit. States are moving on data-centre curbs, which hits the 34% of deals tied to AI.

Clock

In 2006 the market peaked 11 months after the vote. Federal debt is now 121% of GDP and Washington is split, so a rescue would be slower.
